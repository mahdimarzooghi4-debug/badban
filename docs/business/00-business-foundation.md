# Badban Business Foundation

- **Status:** Working Baseline
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Stage:** Business
- **Primary source:** original Badban proposal supplied by the project owner
- **Binding repository decisions:** Decision 0001 and Decision 0002

## 1. Product thesis

Badban is a financial infrastructure for turning approved assets into durable economic security and usable credit capacity for supported households.

The product is built around a simple business chain:

```
Approved Asset
      ↓
Asset Intake / Allocation
      ↓
Ownership & Custody State
      ↓
Valuation
      ↓
Risk Rules / Haircut / Eligibility
      ↓
Backing Value
      ↓
Credit Capacity
      ↓
Loan / Credit
      ↓
Repayment
      ↓
Release / Enforcement
      ↓
Revaluation / Reuse / Growth
```

Badban is **asset-backed**, not gold-specific. Gold is an initial supported Asset Type, not the core domain model.

## 2. Problem being solved

The original proposal identifies four structural problems that remain valid as business drivers:

1. support resources are often consumed rather than preserved as productive capital;
2. households lack durable financial backing for the future;
3. access to credit is constrained by the lack of a traditional guarantor;
4. current support is not sufficiently connected to long-term economic independence.

Badban addresses these problems by preserving and managing an economic base, using that base to create credit capacity, and directing generated value toward current resilience and future independence.

## 3. Mission

Badban's mission is to create and operate a sustainable financial infrastructure that:

- preserves and grows approved backing assets;
- converts backing value into controlled credit capacity;
- enables access to financing without dependence on a traditional personal guarantor;
- creates sustainable financial value that can support livelihood and future resilience;
- maintains transparency, risk controls, and social purpose.

Badban is not, by itself, the full empowerment ecosystem. Training, employment, counseling, market access, and other empowerment services may remain outside its core financial mission.

## 4. Core business outcomes

The evolving Badban model targets four business outcomes:

### 4.1 Durable backing

Each participant has an attributable economic backing position whose state, ownership rights, restrictions, valuation, and availability are explicitly tracked.

### 4.2 Credit access

Approved backing creates a measurable credit capacity under policy-defined valuation and risk rules.

### 4.3 Sustainable financial support

Returns and other permitted economic value generated by the managed asset base may be allocated, under approved policy, to current livelihood support, future financial support, reserve/risk buffers, capital growth, and social reinvestment.

### 4.4 Long-term social capital

The model should avoid one-time consumption of support capital where possible and should allow value to remain useful across time, subject to explicit ownership and exit rules.

## 5. Operating model

Decision 0002 supersedes the original proposal's mandatory split between Badban as guarantor and a separate external lender.

The target customer journey uses a **single operating entity** for:

- approved asset intake, sale, or allocation;
- asset administration;
- ownership/custody state;
- valuation and revaluation;
- backing calculation;
- credit origination;
- disbursement;
- repayment servicing;
- release, restriction, or enforcement of backing.

This is a unified customer and transaction model. Internal separation of duties is still required for governance, risk, audit, fraud prevention, and regulatory implementation.

## 6. Asset model

Decision 0001 establishes a policy-controlled **Asset Type Registry**.

An Asset Type is admissible only when explicitly approved by Badban governance. Each approved Asset Type must define, at minimum:

- eligibility status;
- valuation method;
- authoritative price/value source;
- valuation currency and unit;
- haircut or advance rate;
- liquidity class;
- custody/ownership model;
- revaluation frequency;
- concentration limits;
- backing/collateral treatment;
- release and enforcement rules;
- suspension/deactivation rules.

Adding a new Asset Type should normally be a policy/configuration action, not a redesign of the product core.

## 7. Credit-capacity principle

Credit capacity is derived from **eligible backing value**, not from the nominal amount originally paid or allocated.

Conceptually:

```
Gross Asset Value
  × Eligibility
  × Haircut / Advance Rate
  × Availability Rules
  - Existing Encumbrances
  - Required Risk Buffers
  = Available Credit Capacity
```

The exact formula, limits, rounding, minimums, and portfolio constraints are not yet approved and must be captured as explicit decisions before implementation.

## 8. Economic flows

The original proposal's economic logic remains a working input:

```
Managed Asset Base
      ↓
Economic Return / Value Creation
      ↓
Risk Reserve
      ↓
Policy Allocation
      ├─ Livelihood
      ├─ Future Financial Support
      ├─ Capital Growth
      └─ Social Reinvestment / Charity
```

However, the percentages, entitlement rules, accounting treatment, timing, and legal ownership of each allocation are **not yet fixed**.

The unified lending model adds a second economic flow that must be designed separately:

```
Funding Liquidity
      ↓
Credit Disbursement
      ↓
Outstanding Receivable
      ↓
Repayment
      ↓
Revolving Liquidity / Reserve / Recognized Income
```

The source of lending liquidity and its relationship to the managed asset base are unresolved business decisions.

## 9. Actors

Current business actors are:

- **Participant / Beneficiary** — receives an attributed backing position and financial services.
- **Supporting Institution** — may provide initial capital, eligibility inputs, or program sponsorship.
- **Badban Operating Entity** — operates the integrated asset-backed credit flow.
- **Asset/Custody Function** — controls custody, ownership evidence, settlement, and asset operations; may be internal or delegated.
- **Valuation / Market Data Function** — supplies approved values under policy.
- **Credit & Servicing Function** — originates and services credit within the operating entity.
- **Risk Function** — defines and enforces credit, asset, concentration, liquidity, and default controls.
- **Governance Bodies** — approve policy, risk appetite, admissible Asset Types, and material changes.
- **Control / Audit Function** — independently monitors compliance, accounting integrity, and operational controls.

These are business roles. They do not yet imply a final legal-entity or organizational chart.

## 10. Non-negotiable business controls

The following controls are part of the business baseline:

1. no Asset Type is accepted without explicit policy approval;
2. no credit capacity is created from an unvalued, ineligible, unavailable, or already-encumbered asset position;
3. valuation and credit state must come from authoritative records;
4. asset ownership, custody, encumbrance, and release status must be auditable;
5. a unified operator does not mean one person or one unchecked function controls all steps;
6. risk limits and reserves must be defined before scale;
7. product reporting must make asset value, backing usage, outstanding debt, repayment status, and available capacity transparent;
8. changes to policy that affect financial rights or risk exposure must be versioned and auditable.

## 11. Relationship to the original proposal

The original proposal remains an important source for Badban's social mission, capital-preservation philosophy, wealth-generation engine, risk reserve, governance principles, financial-future concept, and measurement of financial/social impact.

Two parts are now explicitly changed by repository decisions:

- **Gold is not the product model.** It is one configurable Asset Type.
- **A separate external lender is not mandatory for the core flow.** The target model uses a unified asset-and-credit operator.

All other source assumptions remain either working inputs or open questions until separately accepted.

## 12. Open business decisions — next queue

Before Technical architecture begins, the following questions must be resolved and recorded:

1. **Asset ownership model:** who legally owns each asset position, and under what conditions?
2. **Exit rights:** what happens to principal/backing when support ends?
3. **Loan funding model:** where does lending liquidity come from?
4. **Credit product rules:** principal limits, tenor, repayment structure, pricing/fees, grace periods.
5. **Default waterfall:** collection, cure periods, use of reserves, asset enforcement, residual value.
6. **Asset-to-credit formula:** haircuts, LTV/advance rates, concentration and liquidity adjustments.
7. **Return-allocation policy:** livelihood, future, reserve, capital growth, and social reinvestment.
8. **Participant entitlement:** what is owned, withdrawable, transferable, restricted, or only beneficially attributed?
9. **Risk appetite:** portfolio loss limits, liquidity reserve, concentration limits, stress scenarios.
10. **Legal/regulated operating structure:** which licensed or delegated functions are required in the intended jurisdiction.
11. **Accounting model:** recognition of assets, liabilities, receivables, reserves, income, and participant sub-ledgers.
12. **Pilot boundary:** target cohort, initial Asset Type(s), capital size, loan limits, duration, and success metrics.

Until these are resolved, they are not implementation assumptions.

## 13. Definition of Business-stage completion

The Business stage is complete only when:

- the product thesis and target operating model are accepted;
- participant rights and obligations are explicit;
- asset lifecycle is defined;
- credit lifecycle is defined;
- economic flows and funding sources are defined;
- default and loss allocation are defined;
- governance and risk appetite are defined;
- measurable pilot economics and success criteria are approved;
- all material decisions are recorded in the Decision Log.

Only then should Badban advance to the Technical stage.
